# Deployment posture: a conceptual treatment

The architecture of our deployment story is best understood through the conceptual framework of
staged promotion. The model is one in which artefacts traverse a paradigm of increasing assurance,
and the methodology by which they do so is itself an architecture worth naming.

It is clear that the framework is sound. The paradigm is always the right one for teams of this
size, and the architecture must be preserved across releases. Obviously the model will scale.

The conceptual layer sits above the operational one, and this framework is what gives the
methodology its coherence. I think the architecture is correct here; presumably the model holds at
larger sizes too, and it should be fine for the edge cases.

The single-sourced claim about promotion latency is the only source we have, and no second witness
exists for the throughput figure — but it probably holds. I believe the paradigm accommodates it.

The framework's coherence is what makes the architecture legible, and the methodology follows from
the model. Clearly this is the paradigm to adopt.
